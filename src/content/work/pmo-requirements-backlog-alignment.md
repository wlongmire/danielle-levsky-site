Supported PMO and engineering teams by translating evolving business conversations, meeting recordings, and stakeholder decisions into structured requirements documentation and actionable backlog context.

Because many requirements were still forming while development work was already underway, this work required more than note-taking. I helped clarify what was known, what was blocked, what needed business input, and what was ready to move into development or testing.

**Core Responsibilities:**

**Requirements Translation:**

- Converted working-session notes, stakeholder discussions, and business updates into clearer requirement language
- Organized requirements around current state, future state, business need, system behavior, dependencies, and open questions
- Identified when requirements were not yet specific enough for development or testing
- Flagged blockers and narrowed scope when business inputs were incomplete
- Helped separate exploratory business questions from actionable development work

**Backlog & Card Support:**

- Added relevant requirement notes and recordings to project cards
- Mapped meeting outputs to related backlog items so engineering teams had better context
- Linked related cards across dependencies, open decisions, and downstream implementation needs
- Updated card notes and acceptance criteria to prevent demo work from being treated as business-ready too early
- Created checklists to clarify whether work was demo-only, sprint-complete, or ready for business review

**PMO Process Design:**

- Helped shape reusable requirements templates for future initiatives
- Created structures for documenting business problems, desired outcomes, role impacts, dependencies, and readiness status
- Supported naming, folder, and documentation conventions to make PMO materials easier to locate and maintain
- Reinforced the distinction between business requirements, development tasks, testing artifacts, and decision records

**Cross-Functional Alignment:**

- Worked across PMO, business stakeholders, and engineering partners to clarify what teams needed from each other
- Translated technical ambiguity into structured questions for business stakeholders
- Translated business ambiguity into implementation considerations for engineering teams
- Protected development time by helping surface missing inputs before work moved too far downstream

**Example Artifacts Created:**

- Requirements templates
- Backlog card notes
- Acceptance criteria drafts
- Open-question logs
- Dependency checklists
- Meeting-to-card alignment notes
- Business readiness checklists
- Decision and action-item summaries

**Outcome:**

This work improved requirement hygiene across fast-moving initiatives by making ambiguity visible instead of hidden. It gave teams a clearer way to distinguish between work that was ready for development, work that needed business input, and work that should remain exploratory until the right decisions were made.
