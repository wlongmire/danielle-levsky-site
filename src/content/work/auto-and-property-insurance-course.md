I worked as a content developer for Cognitive Arts, a division of NIIT, the largest global provider of learning design and development services. I developed and constructed their courses using Articulate Storyline.

In one project, I designed 39 segments of automobile and property insurance educational courses for customer services representatives at an insurance company. The courses consisted of both face-to-face instruction and digital learning spaces. I designed web-based trainings (WBTs), simulations, scavenger hunts, moderated group discussions, role play guides, and learning assessments. I interviewed Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) to evaluate the success of various curriculums and adjust learning environments. I created the templates my team used within Microsoft Word, DITA-based authoring program Knowledge Delivery, and Articulate Storyline for activity overviews, facilitator notes, role play guides, and WBTs.

We began by introducing an activity overview for each lesson to our shareholders, SMEs, and Content Development Managers (CDMs). Though we were originally given an activity overview template to use, I built and designed an alternate, condensed, and straight-forward version that would be able to provide us with the most concise feedback and verification from all parties. My team then altered an existing feedback form to better serve our needs and provide to our reviewers, who were mostly SMEs employed by our client.

After different versions of the activity overviews were exchanged and approved, I volunteered and developed templates to the facilitator guide, as well as made edits to existing WBT outlines and role play guides for the other instructional designers and content developers to use.

After another review process by the SMEs was completed and approved, the designers and myself built each of our respective lessons using the templates within Knowledge Delivery and Microsoft Word.

After receiving approval from the WBT outlines by the SMEs, I then built various types of WBTs (simulations, decision points, etc.) in Articulate Storyline. I also built a template for the learner guides in Articulate Storyline to allow learners to choose between lessons in each state, depending on their curriculum.

Finally, I developed distance learning guides in Microsoft PowerPoint for each lesson. Once the deliverables were edited and approved through several rounds by managers and SMEs, we compiled each lesson into a course page template I created in Knowledge Delivery. Each lesson could now be used by state or picked à la carte depending on curriculum and facilitator needs.
