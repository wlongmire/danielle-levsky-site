Designed QA/QC and UAT structures to help business and technical teams validate portal functionality in a more organized, scenario-based way.

Rather than treating testing as a final checklist at the end of development, I helped frame UAT as a business-readiness process: What does the user need to do? What should the system allow? What should happen when something is incomplete, incorrect, or blocked? What can be tested now, and what is not yet testable?

**Core Responsibilities:**

**UAT Framework Design:**

- Defined the purpose of UAT in relation to QA/QC and development testing
- Clarified the difference between validating business workflows and identifying technical defects
- Created structures for planning, documenting, and executing UAT sessions
- Helped define what should be tested by business users versus PMO or development teams
- Built testing language around real scenarios instead of only system features

**QA/QC Structure:**

- Outlined test areas for audit review, portal behavior, user actions, completion states, and exception handling
- Identified positive, negative, and edge-case scenarios
- Documented expected results where requirements were known
- Flagged areas that were not fully testable due to incomplete requirements or open business decisions
- Helped prevent unfinished functionality from being treated as validated

**Ownership & Sign-Off:**

- Clarified responsibilities across Business, PMO, and Development
- Positioned PMO as a facilitator, coverage owner, and documentation partner rather than the sole test executor
- Defined when business sign-off was appropriate and when additional clarification was needed first
- Supported a more disciplined approach to deciding whether functionality was ready for broader review

**UAT Session Enablement:**

- Prepared business-facing testing materials
- Structured walkthroughs around user tasks and decision points
- Captured open issues, blockers, and follow-up questions
- Helped keep testing sessions focused on validation rather than drifting into unstructured bug triage

**Example Artifacts Created:**

- QA/QC test plan frameworks
- UAT planning outlines
- Business sign-off models
- Testing ownership matrices
- Scenario-based test suites
- Not-yet-testable markers
- Open-defect and open-question tracking structures

**Outcome:**

The resulting process gave teams a clearer way to approach validation in an environment where requirements, workflows, and system behavior were evolving in parallel. It helped protect UAT integrity by making it clear which workflows were ready to test, which required more business input, and which should not yet be treated as complete.
